Tutorial: from a route to participant and rally master instructions

This walks you through taking a simple route — a start, turn-by-turn instructions and a finish — typing it into the app, and producing a printable set of participant instructions and rally master instructions. It then adds speeds, pauses, checkpoints, out points and transit zones using the sample route. The sheets shown on this page are produced by the app itself from the routes printed here. Wherever you see an orange Richta box, it explains what to take from the rally master sheet for a Richta event.

1. What you need

  • Your route as a list of instructions in order: the start, each turn or landmark, and the finish.
  • A mileage for each instruction. Enter mileages to the nearest 1/1000 mile if you measure that finely; the sheets show and calculate with the value rounded to 1/100 mile. What you type is never changed.
  • The speed for the first leg. A speed stays in effect until a later line gives a new one.
  • A start time for the rally master sheet (set in Rally master settings).

Haven't measured the route yet? Mileages can be left blank — see step 9.

2. Enter a simple route

Type or paste the route into the large text box, one instruction per line, with the fields separated by |:

mileage | instruction | speed | pause | suffix | general

Only the mileage and instruction are needed on most lines; trailing fields can be left off. Here is a simple route:

{{ ex.simple }}
  • 0.00 | START ... | 30 — the mileage, the instruction, and a speed of 30 mph. The first line needs a speed so the app can work out times.
  • 1.20 | Left at stop sign onto Oak Rd — just a mileage and an instruction. The speed stays at 30 until a line gives a new one.
  • 12.00 | FINISH — the finish is an ordinary line.

3. Fill in the title area

Under the text box, enter a Title, an optional Subtitle and the Date. They are printed at the top of every sheet; the title, sheet type and date are also printed in the page footer. You can also put them in the file itself with header lines (Title: ..., Subtitle: ..., Date: 2026-11-21, Start time: 09:30:00); they fill in these fields when a file is loaded.

The Output row has three choices: Participant, Rally master and Measure. The next steps use each in turn.

4. Generate the participant instructions

Choose Participant and click Generate printable output. The sheet opens in a new browser tab.

{{ preview('simple_participant','Participant sheet for the simple route') }}
  • RI # is the route instruction number, then the Mileage and the Instruction.
  • The speed you entered is added to the end of the instruction as CAST 30 (change average speed to).
  • When printed, the type is large and high-contrast with extra space around each row, so it can be read in a moving vehicle.

Click Print at the top of the sheet (the button itself doesn't print), or Download PDF to save it as a PDF with the same layout and its own page numbers. Page numbers appear in the footer in Chrome and Edge (version 131 or newer); in other browsers turn on “Headers and footers” in the print dialog.

5. Generate the rally master instructions

Open Rally master settings under the Output row and check the Start time (09:30:00 here). Because this route has no out point, the times count from the start time. Choose Rally master and click Generate printable output.

7. Checkpoints

A checkpoint is a line with CP in the general field (the last field). Give it its own line, with the instruction text you want the rally master to see and no speed, pause or suffix (participants never see checkpoint lines, so those would not be printed for them):

8.40 | Checkpoint 1 | | | | CP 1

This line is added after 7.80 in the route below.

{{ ex.checkpoint }}

  • On the rally master sheet the checkpoint is highlighted, its RI # column says CP instead of a number, and its CP time is filled in.
  • CP time is the time since the previous checkpoint — or, for the first checkpoint after the start or an out point, the time since that start or out time. Here CP 1 shows {{ facts.cp_time }} ({{ facts.cp_time_dec }} in decimal minutes).

Richta

When you enter a checkpoint in Richta you need two things:

  • Location (latitude and longitude). This is not part of the instructions. You can find it using Google Earth, or mark it during measurement using the Richta app.
  • Perfect time — this is the CP time column on the rally master sheet. The Perfect time and Out time columns on each line are not needed.

A checkpoint timed since the previous checkpoint, the start or an out point — like the one above — is the Richta Since last CP checkpoint type. Out points map to other Richta checkpoint types; see step 8.

8. Odometer check, out points and transit zones

The sample route that opens when you start the app uses the rest of the features. Here it is:

{{ ex.sample }}

Sections

  • [ODOMETER CHECK] — instructions with no speeds, no timing and no checkpoints. It ends with an OUTPOINT line.
  • [RALLY] — the timed instructions (lines with no section header are rally lines).
  • [TRANSIT] — a section that is not timed and has no checkpoints, ending with an OUTPOINT line. Timing restarts at that out point. A transit zone that runs to the finish needs no out point: the sheet shows End of transit zone after the last line.

Instruction numbers carry on through all the sections. Out points are labeled OUT and don't use a number.

Out points

OUTPOINT | mileage | instruction | out time | speed | suffix | general

The out point's speed is the speed for the first leg after it. The out time is one of two types, and participants are shown the matching wording automatically:

Out timeMeaningParticipants see
TOD 09:50Time of day plus car number: car 1 goes out at 09:51, car 2 at 09:52.Out time 9:50 + car number
NEXTMINThe car goes out at the next whole minute after it arrives.Next minute out

You can also put NEXTMIN in the pause field of an ordinary rally line for a next-minute out partway through a leg.

The rally master sheet needs a clock time to count from. The Start time and Car number under Rally master settings supply it: for example with TOD 09:50 and car number 1, the sheet is worked out from 09:51:00. For NEXTMIN you can add the arrival time to assume after the word (NEXTMIN 10:12:50); without it the sheet uses the time the car left the previous timed section.

9. Measuring: the measure sheet

If the mileages aren't known yet, leave them blank and use the Measure output. It shows every line — checkpoints, notes and master-only mileages included — in the participant layout, with an empty mileage cell to write in. It does no calculations, and blank mileages are not an error here.

10. Save your work

Above the text box, Save has three options:

  • Text file — your route exactly as typed, plus the title, subtitle, date and start time as header lines. Load it again with Load file.
  • CSV — the same route as a CSV file you can open in a spreadsheet program. Loading a CSV with the columns mileage, instruction, speed, pause, suffix, general converts it back.
  • Spreadsheet with formulas — a CSV with every input plus formulas for the instruction text, perfect times, out times and checkpoint times. Open it in Excel or Google Sheets, change a mileage, speed or pause (or the start time or car number at the top), and everything recalculates.

Mileages are saved exactly as you entered them.

Checklist

  1. Enter the route (mileage, instruction, speed on the first line), and add checkpoints with CP in the general field.
  2. Fill in the title, subtitle and date, and check the Rally master settings.
  3. Generate and print the Participant sheet.
  4. Generate and print the Rally master sheet.
  5. For Richta: take each checkpoint's CP time from the rally master sheet and its location from Google Earth or the Richta app, and use the Richta checkpoint type shown in step 8.
  6. Save the route (and the spreadsheet if you want a backup).
